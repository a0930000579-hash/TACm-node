1. Background and Problems

Since the emergence of the Proof-of-Work (PoW) consensus mechanism in 2009 has become the most widely adopted consensus algorithm in the blockchain field. However, as network scale expands, the PoW mechanism has exposed a series of fundamental problems:

  1. Computing Power Monopolization: Mining machine manufacturing and mining farm operations show a strong trend of centralization. Small-scale participants struggle to compete fairly, contradicting the original decentralized intention of blockchain.
  2. Energy Waste: Traditional PoW networks consume annual electricity exceeding that of some medium-sized countries. Vast amounts of energy are used for meaningless hash computations, resulting in high social costs.
  3. Participation Barriers: Application-Specific Integrated Circuits (ASICs) are expensive, making it impossible for ordinary users to directly participate in the consensus process. Blockchain has become a game for the few.
  4. Performance Bottlenecks: To ensure security, the PoW mechanism sacrifices transaction processing capacity. legacy PoW chains only process single-digit transactions per second, unable to meet the demands of large-scale commercial applications.

Subsequent consensus mechanisms such as Proof-of-Stake (PoS) and Delegated Proof-of-Stake (DPoS) have improved energy efficiency and performance, but introduced new problems such as capital monopolization, token concentration, and governance centralization. How to achieve a balance between decentralization, security, and performance remains the core challenge in the blockchain field.

TACm proposes a PoT + PoS hybrid consensus mechanism based on Time Warrant, treating "time" — the fairest, non-purchasable resource — as the consensus foundation. Through a decentralized public chain architecture that balances performance and security, TACm provides a new solution for blockchain consensus mechanisms.

3. Consensus Mechanism: Time Warrant and PoT + PoS

3.1 Time Warrant

Time Warrant is the core innovation of TACm's consensus mechanism. Its fundamental idea is to convert a node's online duration into verifiable consensus weight — the longer the online time, the higher the probability of obtaining block production rights. This mechanism is based on the following observations:

  • Time is the only non-purchasable, non-replicable resource. No matter how much computing power or capital one possesses, one cannot obtain more time than anyone else.
  • Continuously online nodes contribute more to the network (forwarding transactions, storing blocks, maintaining network stability) and should receive higher consensus weight.
  • The Time Warrant mechanism naturally defends against Sybil Attacks, as attackers cannot purchase time in bulk.

3.2 Time Bonus Function

A node's time bonus dynamically adjusts with online duration, using a piecewise linear function:

W(t) = 1 + ⌊online time ÷ 6h⌋,    capped at 10
（each 6 online hours adds +1 weight; 10 ≈ 1,000 TACm stake equivalent）

Time-warrant weight accumulates while a node stays online and is computed consistently by on-chain replay (based on block time, not local clock); all nodes syncing the same chain derive identical weights. If continuous interruption exceeds 30 minutes, the bonus resets to its initial value. Time warrant drives block-production weight and node-reward splitting — no energy consumption and no hashrate dependency.

3.3 Time Warrant Hybrid Consensus (PoT+PoS)

TACm is a decentralized public chain that anyone can join without permission. The consensus combines Time Warrant (PoT) with Proof of Stake (PoS), finalized via BFT voting:

  1. Time-Warrant Block Production (PoT): Registered nodes (node:reg) accumulate time-warrant weight by online duration and take turns producing blocks — no hashrate and no capital required.
  2. Staked Validation (PoS): Validators staking ≥1,000 TACm (up to 100) produce blocks and vote by stake weight — independent of PoT node qualification.
  3. Block Validation: After a block is broadcast, the validator set runs BFT voting; the block finalizes once more than 2/3 weight confirms.
  4. Reward Distribution: Each block's coinbase is split on-chain in real time — 45% (nodes) / 15% (pool) / 40% (miners).

3.5 PoT Node Registration and Stake-Free Block Production (node:reg)

Under the Time Warrant design, nodes need no capital to participate in block production. Every node automatically submits a free registration transaction (memo = node:reg, amount 0, fee 0) on startup, writing its public key into the on-chain node registry; as long as it keeps sending liveness heartbeats (hb:on), it is counted into the on-chain online node set and earns Time Warrant block-production weight by online duration: +1 weight per 6 hours online, capped at 10 (roughly equivalent to a 1,000 TACm stake) to prevent pure-time nodes from growing unbounded. Registration and heartbeats are free and replayed from on-chain transactions, so every node syncing the same chain derives the identical PoT node set — this is the complete realization of "all are equal before time": a node does not need to hold 1,000 TACm first to produce blocks.

The bootstrap grant works alongside PoT registration: a new node can claim 1,000 TACm on-chain (limit 50) and be auto-staked to become a staked validator; even without claiming it, the node can still produce blocks purely by its online time — the two qualifications are independent and not mutually binding.

4. Network Architecture

4.1 Layered Architecture

The TACm network adopts a modular layered architecture, divided into four layers from bottom to top:

  1. Consensus Layer: Responsible for block generation, transaction validation, and consensus achievement, implementing the PoT + PoS hybrid consensus with BFT finality.
  2. Network Layer: Responsible for node discovery, transaction broadcasting, and block synchronization, using P2P peer-to-peer communication protocols.
  3. Data Layer: Responsible for persistent storage of block data, transaction data, and state data, supporting relational databases and scalable storage engines.
  4. Application Layer: Responsible for providing external API interfaces and user interfaces, implementing business logic such as exchanges, wallets, and DeFi.

5.3 Transaction Lifecycle

  1. Construction and Signing: The user constructs a transaction and digitally signs it using their private key.
  2. Broadcasting: The transaction is broadcast to the P2P network and enters the transaction memory pool (Mempool).
  3. Packaging: Validator nodes select transactions from the Mempool and package them into blocks, prioritized by fee.
  4. Consensus: The block is confirmed through the PoT + PoS hybrid consensus with BFT voting.
  5. On-Chaining: The block is appended to the end of the blockchain, and transaction states are updated.
  6. Finality: After 6 block confirmations (approximately 18 seconds), the transaction reaches practical finality.

8. Stablecoin Protocol

8.1 TiUSD Overview

TiUSD is the US dollar stablecoin within the TACm ecosystem, pegged 1:1 to the US dollar, designed to provide low-volatility, low-fee value storage and medium of exchange for the ecosystem. TiUSD employs an over-collateralized minting mechanism, where users can mint TiUSD by staking assets such as TACM.

8.2 Stability Mechanisms

  • Over-Collateralization: Every TiUSD is backed by excess crypto asset collateral
  • Arbitrage Mechanism: When TiUSD deviates from $1, arbitrageurs can profit through mint/redeem mechanisms, driving the price back to peg
  • Liquidation Mechanism: When the collateralization ratio falls below the liquidation threshold, the system automatically liquidates collateral assets to repay TiUSD
  • Stability Fee: TiUSD borrowing incurs a stability fee, with fee revenue used for ecosystem development

8.3 TiUSD Applications

TiUSD has a wide range of application scenarios within the TACm ecosystem: as a low-fee trading pair (TiUSD trading pairs enjoy the lowest fees at only 0.5%), as a lending asset in the DeFi lending market, as a target asset for yield aggregators, and as a stable asset safe haven for users to avoid market volatility.

8.4 DAI-style Over-Collateralized CDP (Collateralize TACm, Borrow TiUSD)

TiUSD's over-collateralization is made real by an on-chain CDP (Collateralized Debt Position) engine: any account may lock TACm collateral and mint TiUSD against the on-chain reference price (TAC_TACM_PRICE_USD) with over-collateralization — the same “collateralize → borrow → stability fee → liquidation” lifecycle as DAI, not a simplified 1:1 issuance. When no reference price is configured, the chain refuses to open positions, withdraw collateral, or liquidate, eliminating the risk of operating on fake prices.

9. Governance Mechanism

9.1 Governance Architecture

TACm adopts an On-chain Governance mechanism. TACM holders can obtain governance rights through staking and participate in decisions such as network parameter adjustment, protocol upgrades, and fund allocation. The governance process is fully transparent and traceable.

9.2 Governance Process

  1. Proposal Submission: Users who stake a certain amount of TACM can submit governance proposals
  2. Discussion Period: The proposal enters a 3-day community discussion period to collect feedback and revision suggestions
  3. Voting Period: After the discussion period, a 5-day voting period begins, during which staking users can vote
  4. Tally and Execution: After the voting period, votes are automatically tallied. Proposals receiving more than 50% affirmative votes and reaching quorum are automatically executed
  5. Timelock: Major protocol upgrades are subject to a 48-hour timelock, giving users sufficient reaction time

  • Economic parameters: fee rates, miner reward ratio, ecosystem incentive distribution
  • Financial parameters: lending interest rate models, liquidation thresholds, stablecoin parameters

10. Security Design

10.1 Cryptographic Security

TACm employs industry-standard cryptographic algorithms to ensure network security: transaction signatures use the ECDSA secp256k1 elliptic curve algorithm, block hashing uses SHA-256, and Merkle trees ensure transaction data integrity. User private keys are stored only locally on the user's device; network nodes cannot access user private keys.

10.2 Consensus Security

The PoT + PoS hybrid consensus ensures network security through multiple designs: open public-chain participation with Time Warrant naturally defending against Sybil Attacks (attackers cannot buy time in bulk); 2/3-majority BFT voting among staked validators ensures block finality; continuous online requirements increase attack costs; double-signing detection triggers jail + slash. Theoretically, an attacker would need to control more than 1/3 of validator weight and remain online for a sufficiently long period to launch a successful attack, making the attack cost far higher than the potential gain.

10.3 Smart Contract Security

  • Core contract code is open-source and subject to third-party security audits
  • Major contract upgrades employ a Timelock mechanism
  • Administrative permissions use Multisig control
  • A Bug Bounty program is established to encourage white-hat hackers to discover and report vulnerabilities
  • A Circuit Breaker emergency pause mechanism can suspend contract functionality in extreme situations

10.4 Asset Security

The platform employs a cold/hot wallet separation architecture, with the majority of user assets stored in offline cold wallets, while hot wallets retain only the small amount of funds needed for daily operations. Large withdrawals require multi-signature confirmation. All sensitive operations are recorded in complete audit logs, and abnormal behavior automatically triggers alerts and freezing mechanisms.

13. Developer Ecosystem and Cross-Chain

13.1 Official Developer SDKs

TACm ships official SDKs in Go (sdk/tacclient) and JavaScript (sdk/tacjs) covering key generation, address derivation, transaction signing, and node RPC query/submit. Signing formats are fully interoperable across both languages (whitelisted-field Canonical serialization → DoubleSHA256 → RFC6979 ECDSA → DER), verified by fixed-vector interop tests: transactions signed by tacjs in the browser are validated directly by the Go node, so third-party dApps and wallets can integrate seamlessly.

13.2 Standard JSON-RPC Compatibility Layer (eth_*)

Nodes expose the full eth_* JSON-RPC set (eth_blockNumber, eth_getBalance, eth_getTransactionCount, eth_call, eth_sendRawTransaction, etc.), enabling standard JSON-RPC wallets to connect directly; addresses use the Hash160 standard (tx0 ↔ 0x prefix) and wei amounts are converted to TACm automatically.

13.3 Real Cross-Chain Bridge (TAC ↔ Heterogeneous Chain)

A built-in heterogeneous-chain two-way relayer: TAC assets locked on TAC mint the mapped token on the target chain; burning on the target chain unlocks on TAC. Cross-chain messages are signed and submitted on-chain by the relayer, supporting testnet and mainnet. The bridge supports multi-asset mapping for TACm, TiUSD and USDT (each asset has its own LockProxy and minimum-amount checks; a scan failure on one asset does not block others), with the mapped asset list queried on-chain via /bridge/bsc/status — the goal is interoperability of real assets with mainstream chains, not copying a single asset. Deployment details are in the cross-chain deployment guide.

13.4 Multi-Source Snapshots and Fast Node Sync

New nodes may specify multiple snapshot sources via -snapshot-url / -mainnet-url (comma-separated); endpoints automatically append /api/snapshot. The node tries sources in order and completes the initial state import on the first success, without replaying every block from genesis. Multi-source failover avoids single points of failure and accelerates onboarding of new nodes and validators, supporting permissionless public-chain participation.

13.5 Asset Issuance and Supply Governance

TACm has a hard supply cap of 52,003,300 tokens enforced on-chain; Token Studio supports on-chain issuance of custom tokens and contract calls. The TiUSD stablecoin mints/burns by market capitalization while keeping a minimum circulation of 0.330× of total supply to maintain stability and prevent de-pegging.
